## Please look at these before or soon after launch — legal points

I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice. These are things I noticed while building that I think
your attorney should look at. I have included the specific references so their review is quick and
cheap.

### 3. The guest reviews on the site

**What I need to know:** did anyone ask permission from the guests whose reviews you sent me?

**The situation:** your brief included six lovely reviews from Google and Yelp, each with the
reviewer's full name. I have published the quotes, but I have **changed the credits to
non-identifying** for now — they currently read "Google reviewer, Carlsbad" instead of the person's
name. The real names are safely stored and can be put back immediately if we get the go-ahead.

**Why I did that:** two reasons.
- Yelp's terms of service restrict republishing their review content outside Yelp's own official
  display tools, and Yelp does enforce this. Google reviews belong to the person who wrote them, not
  to the business.
- California law (**Civil Code § 3344**) covers using a person's name in advertising without their
  consent, with statutory damages starting at **$750 per instance**. Six named reviewers used as
  endorsements is six separate exposures.

**The clean fix:** use Yelp's and Google's **official review widgets**, which are licensed exactly for
this purpose. They look great, update automatically, and remove the problem entirely. I am happy to
install them — I just need to know you are comfortable with that route, or alternatively that you have
written permission from each reviewer.

### 4. Who owns the food photography?

**What I need:** written confirmation that Tiger Hospitality Group owns, or is licensed to use, the
photos from the 2023 shoot for commercial use on the website.

**Why it matters:** in the US, hiring a photographer does **not** automatically transfer copyright to
you. Unless there is a signed agreement assigning the rights, the photographer may still own them and
you may hold only a limited licence — which might not cover unlimited website use. The photos are
beautiful and central to the site, so this is worth five minutes to confirm now rather than dealing
with a claim later.

If you have the photographer's contract or invoice, that usually settles it. Please forward it and I
will file it with the project.

### 5. Tiger Hospitality Group's total annual revenue

**What I need:** roughly, is THG's **total** gross annual revenue across all its concepts above or
below **US$25 million**?

**Why I am asking something so blunt:** California's privacy law (CCPA, as amended by CPRA) only
applies to businesses above certain thresholds, and $25M in gross annual revenue is one of them.
Critically, **that threshold is measured for the whole company, not per brand** — so five Lobster Lab
units on their own would not trigger it, but if THG's group total is above $25M then it applies to the
group, including this website.

The difference is real work: above the threshold, the site needs a "Your Privacy Choices" link, plus a
documented process for customers to request access to or deletion of their data. Below it, what we
already have is sufficient.

I genuinely cannot guess this one, and guessing wrong in either direction is bad — over-building wastes
your money, under-building leaves a gap. A simple "above" or "below" from you or Eren is all I need;
I do not need the actual figure.

**Also worth telling your attorney, all good news:**
- I have published a **Privacy Policy** and **Terms of Use** on the site. The privacy policy was
  legally required as soon as the catering form started collecting names, phone numbers and email
  addresses from California residents (**Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 22575**) — there was previously none,
  so this was a real gap and it is now closed.
- I added an **optional, unchecked** consent box for marketing text messages on the catering form.
  Agreeing to be contacted about a catering request is not the same as agreeing to marketing texts,
  and under the TCPA marketing texts need separate express written consent. Keeping them separate
  protects you.
- **Calorie labelling is not required for you.** Federal menu-labelling rules kick in at **20 or more
  locations**; at five you are exempt. Worth knowing for when you expand — I have built the menu data
  so nutrition information can be added later without a rebuild.
- Please double-check your **Proposition 65** signage at each location is correct for seafood
  (mercury) and fried items (acrylamide). That is an on-premises posting requirement rather than a
  website one, but it came up while I was reviewing the menus.
- I have also written an **accessibility statement** and built the site to WCAG 2.1 AA standards. I
  would recommend commissioning a formal accessibility audit within a few months of launch —
  California sees a high volume of website accessibility claims against restaurants, and having a
  dated audit plus a record of fixes is the practical protection.

### 7. Smaller menu PDF files

**What I need:** a web-optimised export of the food-hall menu PDF.

**Why:** the current file is **10 MB**. On a phone using mobile data that can take a long time to open,
and a lot of people give up before it loads — right at the moment they were deciding what to eat. The
Sky Deck menu is 3.6 MB, which is more reasonable.

Whoever made the PDF can usually fix this in one step by exporting for "smallest file size" or "web".
If that is not possible, I can compress it here — it just loses a little image quality, so I would
rather you send a proper export.

**Related suggestion for later:** at some point I would like to put the menu on the website as a proper
web page instead of only a PDF. Three reasons: it loads instantly on phones, Google can actually read
it (so you start showing up for searches like "lobster roll near me", which a PDF does not help with),
and it works properly for guests using screen readers. It is a bigger piece of work, so I have put it
on the roadmap rather than the launch list — but it is probably the single biggest thing we could do to
bring in new customers.
