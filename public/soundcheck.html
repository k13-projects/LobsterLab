What we need: the ordering page web address for each of your five locations.

Why it matters. We only have the Toast link for Carlsbad. Someone standing in Little Italy who taps ORDER ONLINE would be sent to the Carlsbad store, so they order from the wrong place or give up. Your brief listed DoorDash and Grubhub, but the links were left as the word “Link”. Delivery is often 20 to 40 percent of orders for a food‑hall format, so this is real revenue sitting idle.

2

Where catering inquiries should go

What we need: an inbox for catering inquiries, and a free Formspree account.

Why it matters. This is the most important item on the list commercially. Catering is your highest‑value order. One corporate lunch is worth many walk‑ins, and it is the one channel where you keep the full margin instead of paying a platform a commission. The form is built and working, with exactly the fields your brief specified, but it has nowhere to send submissions. If someone fills it in today it tells them to email you instead, and a good number will not bother. Every day this stays unconnected is lost catering pipeline.

Why a shared inbox. If inquiries go to one person and they are away or leave, the leads disappear. A shared address survives staff changes.

3

The guest reviews on the site

What we need to know: did anyone ask permission from the guests whose reviews you sent?

The situation. Your brief included six lovely reviews from Google and Yelp, each with the reviewer’s full name. We published the quotes but changed the credits to non‑identifying for now, so they read “Google reviewer, Carlsbad” instead of the person’s name. The real names are stored safely and can go back immediately with the go‑ahead.

Why. Yelp’s terms restrict republishing review content outside their own display tools, and they enforce it. Google reviews belong to whoever wrote them, not to the business. Separately, California Civil Code § 3344 covers using a person’s name in advertising without consent, with statutory damages starting at $750 per instance. Six named reviewers is six separate exposures.

The clean fix. Use Yelp’s and Google’s official review widgets, which are licensed for exactly this. They look good, update themselves, and remove the problem entirely.

4

Who owns the food photography?

What we need: written confirmation that Tiger Hospitality Group owns, or is licensed to use, the 2023 shoot for commercial use on the website.

Why. In the US, hiring a photographer does not automatically transfer copyright. Without a signed agreement assigning rights, the photographer may still own them and you may hold only a limited licence that does not cover unlimited website use. The photos are central to the site, so this is worth five minutes now rather than a claim later. If you have the contract or invoice, forward it and we will file it with the project.

5

Tiger Hospitality Group’s total annual revenue

What we need: roughly, is THG’s total gross annual revenue across all concepts above or below US$25 million?

Why we are asking something so blunt. California’s privacy law (CCPA, as amended by CPRA) only applies above certain thresholds, and $25M gross annual revenue is one of them. Critically, that threshold is measured for the whole company, not per brand. Five Lobster Lab units alone would not trigger it, but if THG’s group total is above $25M then it applies to the group, including this website.

The difference is real work. Above it, the site needs a “Your Privacy Choices” link plus a documented process for customers to request access to or deletion of their data. Below it, what we have is sufficient. Guessing wrong either way is bad: over‑building wastes your money, under‑building leaves a gap. A simple above or below is all we need, not the figure.

Also worth telling your attorney, all good news.

A Privacy Policy and Terms of Use are now published. The privacy policy became legally required the moment the catering form started collecting names, phone numbers and emails from California residents (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 22575). There was none before, and that gap is now closed.

The catering form has an optional, unchecked consent box for marketing texts. Agreeing to be contacted about a catering request is not the same as agreeing to marketing, and under the TCPA marketing texts need separate express written consent.

Calorie labelling is not required for you. Federal rules start at 20 or more locations. At five you are exempt, and we built the menu data so nutrition can be added later without a rebuild.

Please double‑check your Proposition 65 signage at each location covers seafood (mercury) and fried items (acrylamide). That is an on‑premises posting requirement rather than a website one, but it came up while reviewing the menus.

There is an accessibility statement and the site is built to WCAG 2.1 AA. We would recommend a formal accessibility audit within a few months of launch. California sees a high volume of website accessibility claims against restaurants, and a dated audit plus a record of fixes is the practical protection.

7

Smaller menu PDF files

What we need: a web‑optimised export of the food‑hall menu PDF.

Why. The current file is 10 MB. On mobile data that takes a long time to open and many people give up before it loads, right at the moment they were deciding what to eat. The Sky Deck menu is 3.6 MB, which is more reasonable. Exporting for “smallest file size” or “web” usually fixes it in one step. If that is not possible we can compress it here, but it loses a little image quality, so we would rather have a proper export.

A suggestion for later. At some point we would like the menu as a proper web page rather than only a PDF. It loads instantly on phones, Google can actually read it (so you start appearing for searches like “lobster roll near me”, which a PDF does not help with), and it works for guests using screen readers. It is a bigger piece of work, so it sits on the roadmap rather than the launch list, but it is probably the single biggest thing we could do to bring in new customers.

Why we are asking. Going back through everything you sent, we found your original LOBSTER LAB, WEBSITE IDEAS deck.
